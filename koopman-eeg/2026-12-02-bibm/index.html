1 The lens

Deep Koopman lets a network find the right coordinates by itself

Encoder, one linear step, decoder

Lusch, Kutz and Brunton trained the encoder so that one matrix moves the state forward. In epilepsy, the idea has so far steered stimulation, not predicted seizures.

Lusch, Kutz and Brunton, Deep learning for universal linear embeddings of nonlinear dynamics, Nat Commun 2018Liang et al. 2023
Read the figure left to right: the network maps a state into new coordinates (phi), one matrix K moves it forward, the decoder maps it back. For real data we do not know the right coordinates, so we learn them. Liang and colleagues used an online deep Koopman model for closed loop neurostimulation in epilepsy. Nobody had used the learned spectrum itself to predict.

2 KSNet

Follow ten seconds of patient chb01 through KSNet

Real clip: CHB-MIT chb01_03, 2396 to 2406 s, eight of 23 channels shown
Step 1: slide a 1/8 second window along the clip, each snapshot is encoded into 16 numbers per channel. Step 2: one shared matrix K, 16 by 16, pushes the state forward one step, then again and again, three steps. Step 3: decode each predicted state and compare it with what the EEG actually did. The loss says: K must predict the future, not only describe the present. Training on three steps instead of one adds up to 0.02 per metric. K is a full matrix, so rhythms can couple and rotate.
2 KSNet

A learnable band decides which rhythms the classifier gets to hear

Each dot is a rhythm of the learned K

Angle gives its frequency, distance from the center how long it lasts. A soft band with learned edges weights the rhythms, then a small ConvNet classifies.

Shaded: an example band. These are rhythms of the learned state, not EEG frequency bands.

KSNet, Section III.C
These are the real 16 eigenvalues the model learned for patient chb01, read off our paper figure. Frequency of a rhythm equals its angle times 256 over 2 pi. The band is soft, two sigmoids, and its edges are trained with everything else. Conjugate pairs get equal weight, so the representation stays real. Careful with the words: a 40 Hz rhythm of the latent state is not 40 Hz gamma in the EEG, because the encoder is nonlinear.

3 Results

The Koopman space alone loses. Choosing the rhythms wins

KSNet ablation, Fig. 4
Ask the room first: if we remove the band and feed all rhythms to the classifier, where does it land? Most people guess just below KSNet. Click: it lands below the best baseline, 0.69 on CHB-MIT, 0.72 on SIENA. Click: with the band, 0.81 and 0.82. A linear dynamical space is not enough by itself. The selection of rhythms carries the gain.

Before a seizure, the fast learned rhythms barely fade

One model trained only on pre-ictal clips, one only on inter-ictal. Vertical: decay rate, 0 means no fading. CHB-MIT, six folds.

KSNet, Fig. 3. SIENA shows differences at other frequencies, see backup.
Payoff of the slider. Blue: models that only saw pre-ictal EEG. Red: only inter-ictal. Each point is the average decay rate of the learned rhythms in an 8 Hz bin. 3 The catch

On a patient the model has never seen, prediction falls to chance

Every brain plays its own rhythms

AUC when one patient is held out entirely. The model predicts pre-ictal too often for unseen patients.

The objective: reconstruct, predict three steps ahead, classify

L = λ1 Lrecon + λ2 Lpred + Lcls

Reconstruction keeps the information in the state. Prediction decodes φ(Xτ)Kh and compares it with Xτ+h for h = 1, 2, 3. Classification: binary cross entropy.

Mode frequency fi = |arg λi| / (2πΔt). Decay rate Re μi with μi = log λi / Δt. Band weight σ(c(fi − flow)) σ(c(fhigh − fi)), normalized to sum to one.

Snapshots of 32 samples, latent size 16, encoder and decoder two layers of 64, Adam 0.001, λ1 = λ2 = 1.

Data and protocol

CHB-MIT: 23 patients after excluding chb24, 256 Hz, 23 channels. SIENA: 13 patients after excluding PN00, downsampled from 512 to 256 Hz, 17 channels. Unfiltered, full band to 128 Hz.

Pre-ictal: the 30 min before onset. Inter-ictal: at least 30 min from any seizure and at least 1 h before the next. 100 random 10 s segments per patient, 50 per class.

Segment level stratified 5 fold split, about 8 to 1 to 1. Leave one patient out: three splits per held out patient.
