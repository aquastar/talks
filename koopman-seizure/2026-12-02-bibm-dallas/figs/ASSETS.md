## Quotes

All copied verbatim from the fetched abstract or page. PubMed abstracts were fetched with the PubMed MCP tool (get_article_metadata).

1. "The sudden and apparently unpredictable nature of seizures is one of the most disabling aspects of the disease epilepsy."
   Mormann F, Andrzejak RG, Elger CE, Lehnertz K. Seizure prediction: the long and winding road. Brain 2007, 130(Pt 2):314-33. doi:10.1093/brain/awl241. Fetched: PubMed PMID 17008335.
2. "Since the 1970s investigations on the predictability of seizures have advanced from preliminary descriptions of seizure precursors to controlled studies applying prediction algorithms to continuous multi-day EEG recordings."
   Same source as 1.
3. "While most of the studies published in the 1990s and around the turn of the millennium yielded rather promising results, more recent evaluations could not reproduce these optimistic findings, thus raising a debate about the validity and reliability of previous investigations."
   Same source as 1.
4. "In none of these works, however, the performance of the seizure prediction statistics is tested against a null hypothesis, an otherwise ubiquitous concept in science."
   Andrzejak RG, Mormann F, Kreuz T, Rieke C, Kraskov A, Elger CE, Lehnertz K. Testing the null hypothesis of the nonexistence of a preseizure state. Phys Rev E 2003, 67:010901. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.67.010901. Fetched: PubMed PMID 12636484.
5. "Here, we propose the technique of seizure time surrogates based on a Monte Carlo simulation to remedy this deficit."
   Same source as 4.
6. "Complex dynamical systems, ranging from ecosystems to financial markets and the climate, can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur."
   Scheffer M, Bascompte J, Brock WA, et al. Early-warning signals for critical transitions. Nature 2009, 461:53-9. doi:10.1038/nature08227. Fetched: PubMed PMID 19727193.
7. "This phenomenon has traditionally been investigated by linearizing the equations of flow and testing for unstable eigenvalues of the linearized problem, but the results of such investigations agree poorly in many cases with experiments."
   Trefethen LN, Trefethen AE, Reddy SC, Driscoll TA. Hydrodynamic stability without eigenvalues. Science 1993, 261:578-84. doi:10.1126/science.261.5121.578. Fetched: PubMed PMID 17758167.
8. "Koopman spectral theory has emerged as a dominant perspective over the past decade, in which nonlinear dynamics are represented in terms of an infinite-dimensional linear operator acting on the space of all possible measurement functions of the system."
   Brunton SL, Budišić M, Kaiser E, Kutz JN. Modern Koopman Theory for Dynamical Systems. arXiv:2102.12086 (SIAM Review 2022, 64(2)). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12086 (arXiv abstract, may differ slightly from the SIAM Review version).
9. "This linear representation of nonlinear dynamics has tremendous potential to enable the prediction, estimation, and control of nonlinear systems with standard textbook methods developed for linear systems."
   Same source as 8.
10. "This work leverages deep learning to discover representations of Koopman eigenfunctions from data."
    Lusch B, Kutz JN, Brunton SL. Deep learning for universal linear embeddings of nonlinear dynamics. Nat Commun 2018, 9:4950. doi:10.1038/s41467-018-07210-0. Fetched: PubMed PMID 30470743 (published version. The arXiv 1712.09707 abstract words it differently).
11. "An overwhelming majority of people with epilepsy regard the unpredictability of seizures as a major issue."
    Kuhlmann L, Lehnertz K, Richardson MP, Schelter B, Zaveri HP. Seizure prediction, ready for a new era. Nat Rev Neurol 2018, 14:618-630. doi:10.1038/s41582-018-0055-2. Fetched: PubMed PMID 30131521.
12. "A highly influential review published in 2007 concluded that insufficient evidence indicated that seizures could be predicted."
    Same source as 11.
13. "Unfortunately, surgical success rates vary between 30 and 70% because no clinically validated biological marker of the SOZ exists."
    Li A, Huynh C, Fitzgerald Z, et al., Sarma SV. Neural fragility as an EEG marker of the seizure onset zone. Nat Neurosci 2021, 24:1465-1474. doi:10.1038/s41593-021-00901-w. Fetched: PubMed PMID 34354282.
14. "Fragility predicts 43 out of 47 surgical failures, with an overall prediction accuracy of 76% compared with the accuracy of clinicians at 48% (successful outcomes)."
    Same source as 13.
15. "Theoretical interpretations considering the brain as a dynamical system suggest that prior to a seizure, recorded brain signals may exhibit critical slowing down, a warning signal preceding many critical transitions in dynamical systems."
    Maturana MI, Meisel C, Dell K, et al., Cook MJ, Kuhlmann L, Freestone DR. Critical slowing down as a biomarker for seizure susceptibility. Nat Commun 2020, 11:2172. doi:10.1038/s41467-020-15908-3. Fetched: PubMed PMID 32358560.
16. "These results provide strong validation of theoretical models and demonstrate that critical slowing down is a reliable indicator that could be used in seizure forecasting algorithms."
    Same source as 15.
17. "Using a surrogate-based evaluation procedure of sensitivity and specificity of time-resolved estimates of early warning indicators, we found no evidence for critical slowing down prior to 105 epileptic seizures."
    Wilkat T, Rings T, Lehnertz K. No evidence for critical slowing down prior to human epileptic seizures. Chaos 2019, 29:091104. doi:10.1063/1.5122759. Fetched: PubMed PMID 31575122.
18. "Around 50 million people worldwide have epilepsy, making it one of the most common neurological diseases globally."
    World Health Organization. Epilepsy fact sheet, 7 February 2024. Fetched: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/epilepsy (Key facts).
19. "It is estimated that up to 70% of people living with epilepsy could live seizure-free if properly diagnosed and treated."
    Same source as 18 (Key facts).
20. "The risk of premature death in people with epilepsy is up to three times higher than for the general population."
    Same source as 18 (Key facts).
21. "The unpredictability of the occurrence of epileptic seizures contributes to the burden of the disease to a major degree."
    Aschenbrenner-Scheibe R, Maiwald T, Winterhalder M, Voss HU, Timmer J, Schulze-Bonhage A. How well can epileptic seizures be predicted? An evaluation of a nonlinear method. Brain 2003, 126(Pt 12):2616-26. doi:10.1093/brain/awg265. Fetched: PubMed PMID 14506067.
22. "The ability to forecast seizures is a significant unmet need and would have a transformative effect on the lives of people living with epilepsy."
    Janse SA, Dumanis SB, Huwig T, Hyman S, Fureman BE, Bridges JFP. Patient and caregiver preferences for the potential benefits and risks of a seizure forecasting device: A best-worst scaling. Epilepsy Behav 2019, 96:183-191. doi:10.1016/j.yebeh.2019.04.018. Fetched: PubMed PMID 31150998 (survey of 493 respondents).
